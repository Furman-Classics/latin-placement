--- Conversation: Question Selection Algorithm (2026-03-19) ---

Q: How does the question selection algorithm work?

The server uses stratified sampling by chapter number as a difficulty proxy.
Questions are split into easy (ch ≤10), medium (ch 11–20), hard (ch ≥21).
Each tier is independently Fisher-Yates shuffled, then sliced to a fixed count:
  Part 1: 14 easy, 13 medium, 8 hard (35 total)
  Part 2: 10 easy, 9 medium, 6 hard (25 total)
The 60 combined questions are shuffled again before sending to the client.
`correct` is stripped server-side before the client receives them.

Q: Are there better methods?

- Per-question difficulty tags (more precise than chapter number)
- Weighted sampling (bias toward important topics)
- Item Response Theory (IRT) — calibrate per-question difficulty from real response data
- Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) — real-time adaptive selection
- Content balancing by grammar topic
- Enemy item tracking (prevent hint pairs)

Q: What would CAT look like here?

Architecture: instead of sending all 60 upfront, the server drives question-by-question.
  POST /api/start → question 1
  POST /api/answer → score, update ability estimate θ, return next question
  POST /api/done → return final placement

Requires server-side session state (in-memory Map is fine for low concurrency).
Selection: pick unused question whose difficulty is closest to current θ.
Stopping rules: fixed length (~20–30 q), confidence threshold on θ, or chapter ceiling.
Ability estimator: even a basic EAP calculation is ~20 lines.

Tradeoffs: shorter test, no skip/review, more complexity.
Verdict: stateless 60-question approach is fine for a once/twice-yearly exam with <100 students.
CAT earns its complexity for continuous or high-volume use.
